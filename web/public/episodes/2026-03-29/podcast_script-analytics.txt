Amir: That's a great question, Dana. While OCR, Optical Character Recognition, is definitely a foundational component of IDP, it's much, much more than that. OCR just converts images of text into machine-readable text. IDP goes significantly further: it uses AI, including machine learning and now increasingly large language models, to *understand* the content of those documents. It's about extracting structured information from unstructured or semi-structured documents, classifying them by type, and even validating or verifying the extracted data.

Dana: Okay, so it's about context and meaning, not just characters. What kind of documents are we talking about here? Invoices, legal contracts, medical records, financial statements? The kind of paper-based stuff that still plagues many businesses?

Amir: All of the above! Think about the sheer volume of unstructured text data businesses deal with daily: invoices, purchase orders, contracts, medical claims, insurance forms, research papers, customer feedback, emails, regulatory filings. Traditionally, processing these documents was manual, slow, and highly error-prone. IDP automates this. For example, it can extract the vendor name, invoice number, line items, and total amount from an invoice, even if the layout varies considerably.
